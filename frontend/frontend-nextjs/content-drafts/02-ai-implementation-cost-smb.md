# AI Implementation Cost: Honest Numbers for Small and Medium Businesses

Here is the uncomfortable truth most AI vendors will not tell you upfront: the software licence is the cheap part.

If you are running a business with twenty to two hundred people, you have probably heard wildly different numbers. One consultant says five figures a month. Another says "start with a free trial." Neither is being entirely honest. The real cost of AI implementation sits somewhere in between, and it depends far more on your operations than on whichever tool you pick.

Let us walk through the actual line items. Not projections. Not "it depends." Actual costs, drawn from real implementations.

## The software licence: £40 to £500 a month

This is the number everyone asks about first, and it is the least important one. Platform licences for AI operations tools range from roughly thirty pounds a month for a basic operational workspace to a few hundred pounds for a full business licence with governed agents and workflow automation.

That is table stakes. The real costs sit upstream and downstream of the licence.

## The hidden costs that catch people off guard

### Integration labour

Connecting your AI platform to the tools your team actually uses, your CRM, your email, your project management, your internal database, rarely happens in an afternoon. Even with pre-built connectors, someone has to configure permissions, map data fields, and test the flow.

Budget: anywhere from a few hundred to a couple of thousand pounds in setup time, depending on how many systems you are connecting and how clean your APIs already are.

### Data preparation

This is the one that surprises everyone. Before an AI agent can triage your support emails, someone has to structure the historical data it learns from. If your last three years of customer conversations live across three different tools and two file formats, expect a week or two of cleanup work, whether you do it in house or pay someone.

Budget: one to three weeks of a capable person's time. This is usually the single largest line item in a first AI deployment.

### Training and change management

The model works fine out of the box. The humans need more work. Your team needs to understand what the AI can and cannot do, where to check its outputs, and how to handle edge cases. Skip this and you will have a perfectly functional system that nobody uses.

Budget: a few hours of structured onboarding per team, plus a couple of weeks of light-touch monitoring once the system is live.

## The honest cost range

For a typical small to medium business deploying its first AI agent, the total cost breaks down roughly like this:

| Line item | Range |
|---|---|
| Platform licence (monthly) | £40 to £500 |
| Integration setup (one time) | £200 to £2,000 |
| Data preparation (one time) | £500 to £2,500 |
| Training and onboarding (one time) | £200 to £1,000 |

That puts a first deployment somewhere between one thousand and five thousand pounds to get off the ground, plus a monthly licence. After the first deployment, additional agents and workflows cost a fraction of that because the infrastructure is already in place.

## What you should not be spending money on

Consultants who charge by the hour to "discover" your needs without delivering anything concrete. Proprietary model training you do not need (off-the-shelf models work fine for most business operations). Custom development that reinvents wheels already available in modern platforms.

## The real price of doing nothing

The cost most businesses overlook is the status quo. If manual processes are consuming fifteen hours of staff time per week, the monthly cost of not automating is somewhere between five hundred and two thousand pounds in labour alone. Against that baseline, even the upper end of the implementation range pays for itself within a quarter.

Start small. Pick one process. Measure the before and after. Let the numbers make the case for the next one.
